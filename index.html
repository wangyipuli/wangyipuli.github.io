Biography

I am a PhD student in Forestry at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. Before moving to Madison I completed an M.Eng. in Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing at Peking University, advised by Prof. Yang Hong, and a B.S. in Applied Meteorology at China Agricultural University under Prof. Jing Wang.

My research centres on evapotranspiration — the return of water from land to atmosphere, and the single largest term in the terrestrial water budget after precipitation. Much of my work has gone into the unglamorous but load-bearing part of the problem: the observations themselves. Eddy-covariance records are chronically incomplete, so I built a framework that reconstructs continuous in-situ ET series without relying on other ground measurements, and used it to release the first benchmark dataset for global ET estimation from FLUXNET2015. More recently I have been training attention-based deep learning models — pre-trained on 560 million samples, fine-tuned across 400+ flux sites — to estimate ET at hourly resolution.

A good deal of my education has happened outdoors rather than at a desk: installing eddy-covariance systems, taking radiation measurements, and growing the crops the instruments end up sitting over. It is a useful corrective — it is harder to take a gap-filled time series for granted once you have stood in the field where the gap came from.
