About

Wangyipu Li is a PhD student in the Department of Forest and Wildlife Ecology at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. His research interests cover vegetation dynamic monitoring, estimation of water-carbon fluxes, and issues related to Earth system science. He earned a M.Eng. in Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing at Peking University, and a B.S. in Applied Meteorology at China Agricultural University.

Projects and Research Experiences

Hourly Evapotranspiration Estimation
Master's thesis · Peking University
Jun 2025 — present
  • Architected and implemented an attention-based deep learning model for hourly ET estimation.
  • Assembled multi-source datasets for pre-training (560M samples) and fine-tuning (400+ flux sites).
  • Trained the model on HPC under a pre-train/fine-tune strategy and validated the results.
Patterns of Surface Energy Allocation (LE/Rn)*
Graduate research · Peking University
Oct 2024 — Jun 2025
  • Collected global surface flux data across models and ran trend and attribution analyses.
  • Characterised spatiotemporal patterns of energy partitioning and identified the dominant controls across ecosystems.
Post-processing of Surface Water Flux Observations*
Master's thesis · Peking University
Nov 2022 — Oct 2024
  • Designed a framework that fills gaps in and extends ET ground-observation records without depending on other in-situ measurements.
  • Validated the framework and produced the first benchmark dataset for global ET estimation, published in Earth System Science Data.
GPP Dynamics and Drivers
Undergraduate research · China Agricultural University
Dec 2021 — Dec 2022
  • Funded by the Beijing Undergraduate Research Program (¥8,000); rated Excellent — 2 of 15 projects.
  • Quantified the spatiotemporal dynamics of GPP and its drivers across agro-pastoral ecotones through trend, correlation and sensitivity analyses.
  • Showed that the facilitative effect of precipitation on vegetation growth can be offset by high-temperature stress.
